Problem 1 — The AI Front Door

The employee AI front door is split in two, and the security levels don't match.

  • ServiceNow bought MoveWorks for $2.85B (2025) to be the AI chat front door for employees.[2]
  • Its new bundle, "Employee Works" (MoveWorks + Employee Service Center), forces a choice: buy MoveWorks now, or wait for an integration plan that does not exist.
  • An independent analyst noted MoveWorks is built for general commercial use and lacks models for regulated sectors like government.[2]
The FedRAMP gap
MODERATEMoveWorks GovCloud FedRAMP — only since Feb 2026 [3]
HIGHServiceNow core platform (GCC) FedRAMP [4]

An agency that requires the High security level cannot run MoveWorks at that level without compromising its setup.

Salesforce advantage

Employees use the tool they already have — Slack or Microsoft Teams — plus an included self-service portal with an AI chatbot, all at the FedRAMP High level on Government Cloud Plus. No product to choose between, and no security-level gap.[4][7]

Problem 2 — "Out-of-the-Box" Agents

Their "out-of-the-box" agents don't actually work out of the box.

  • Not usable as delivered — heavy setup is required before anything runs.
  • Need constant hands-on help from ServiceNow or a partner.
  • Wrapped in proprietary system prompts and code that prove ineffective: teams spend heavy time rewriting the agent instructions, and results come back wildly inconsistent from one run to the next.
  • The "out-of-the-box agent" turns into another multi-month, services-heavy project — the same story as ITOM.
Question to ask

Of the out-of-the-box agents you purchased, how many are actually live in production today without paying for a services project to set them up?

Salesforce approach

Agents built on the customer's own data, running where employees already work, instead of coded to order in each environment.

Problem 3 — ITOM

ITOM is expensive to buy and very hard to deploy.

Across federal and DoD, only a handful of customers ever get ITOM set up correctly. Plenty buy it. A few start. Almost none finish — it takes a large budget and hard-to-find expertise.

ServiceNow's own community is full of teams asking how to run Discovery across hundreds of thousands of assets; specialists report most Discovery projects stall.[1]

The Salesforce advantage

The CMDB comes with the platform, not a separate ITOM project

CMDB, Discovery and the Service Graph are part of Agentforce IT Service, so there's no separate ITOM product to license just to get a working CMDB.[11]

Question to ask

Is the ITOM they bought actually deployed, and can they produce a current CMDB report today?

Problem 4 — Upgrades & Cost

Renewal costs climb through forced, self-managed upgrades.

Upgrades are a project, not a switch

Each ServiceNow upgrade is self-managed and regression-tested. Most federal customers take only one of the two yearly releases, so they stay perpetually ~6 months behind on features they've already paid for.[5]

Salesforce advantage

Automatic seasonal releases, no upgrade project, and predictable per-user pricing.[11][15]

The Cost Advantage — CMDB

In ServiceNow the CMDB needs a separate ITOM product. In Salesforce it comes with the platform.

ServiceNow

A working CMDB means licensing ITOM on top of ITSM

The CMDB structure ships with the platform, but keeping it current takes Discovery / Service Graph — a separate ITOM product, metered per discovered infrastructure item and widely reported as expensive to own over time.[12][13]

Salesforce

CMDB, Discovery & Service Graph come with the platform

They're part of Agentforce IT Service, not a separately-licensed ITOM add-on. Only discovered infrastructure items are metered.[14]

Salesforce advantage

Federal buyers price the whole path to a working CMDB. With ServiceNow that's ITSM plus a separate ITOM project; with Salesforce the CMDB and Service Graph are already in the subscription.[14]
